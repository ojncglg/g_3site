G3 Industries Product Briefing

A plain-language overview of the G3 public safety operations platform.

G3 is a modular software platform for police agency operations. It helps agencies organize leave, staffing, assignments, field records, public input, and command visibility in one structured environment.

How to read this briefing

This document is informational. It explains what each module does, what problem it addresses, and how the workflow looks from the officer, supervisor, and command staff perspectives.

02 / Problem

Why these workflows matter

Many agencies still rely on paper forms, spreadsheets, and disconnected tools for work that directly affects staffing, accountability, liability, and officer safety. That creates more than inconvenience. When supervisors and command staff cannot clearly see staffing levels, availability, coverage gaps, and expected turnover for a specific day, the agency is left making operational decisions with incomplete information. G3 reduces that guesswork by turning staffing activity into a clearer, more reliable operating picture.

Officer perspective

Officers need a simple way to check their schedule, see available vacation time, track request status, and access opportunities fairly without relying on informal handoffs.

Supervisor perspective

Supervisors need to approve, assign, verify, and document without checking multiple systems or rebuilding context manually.

Command perspective

Command staff need visibility into staffing risk, operational readiness, approvals, trends, and audit history.

Agency impact

Manual workflows cost time, create inconsistency, weaken accountability, and make it harder to explain decisions later.

03 / Modules

Module overview

Each module tackles a specific law enforcement problem. One module can improve one workflow, but the modules work even better together by sharing staffing, roster, and assignment context.

Vacation Book

Leave requests, approvals, calendars, coverage, daily rosters, units, squads, shifts, statuses, pairings, assignments, staffing graphs that forecast low-staff windows, least-privilege access, and audit history.

Vacation Bidding

Supports seniority or lottery-based vacation bidding, including separate bidding lanes, defined bidding seasons, selection windows, policy caps, fair resolution, and automatic calendar placement.

Extra Duty / Pay Jobs

Public intake, supervisor vetting, fair assignments, conflict checks, billing exports, and audit trails.

Training Tracker

Supports training day planning, availability checks, roster impact, and assignment visibility while giving officers clear instructions on where to report and helping the training unit track attendance.

Tow Logs

Tow companies are assigned unit designators and can submit tow sheets with the required information for private tows and repossessions. The officer's role is simplified to running the vehicle through NCIC and verifying the entry.

Anonymous Tips

Secure public input, supervisor triage, tasking, status tracking, and audit-ready reporting.

04 / Details

Module briefings

The following cards explain each module in a consistent format: purpose, common problem, role impact, and operational improvement.

Scheduling and leave

Vacation Book

Vacation Book manages leave requests, approvals, availability, calendar placement, daily roster impact, unit and squad views, shift status, pairings, assignments, access controls, and audit history.

Common problem
  • Daily schedules may live in paper forms, emails, texts, or spreadsheets.
  • Officers cannot request time off when they are away from headquarters.
  • Supervisors often check staffing manually before approving time off.
  • Supervisors are unable to see coverage gaps for turnover.
  • Command may not see future staffing issues until they are close.
  • Command staff may not see staffing issues until there is an incident.
  • Rosters are often updated in one place but referenced in several others.
  • Status changes such as sick, training, vacation, detail, or unavailable can be missed.
  • Different ranks need different levels of access and editing authority.
Officer Submits requests, sees status, and views assignments in a cleaner daily schedule.
Supervisor Gets a complete view of staffing, schedules, available assets, rules, pairings, assignments, and audit history so they can approve requests with control and confidence.
Command Gets visibility into leave patterns, roster impact, staffing status, and upcoming shortages across squads, shifts, and units.
Annual selection process

Vacation Bidding

This module helps command staff turn a painful manual process into a structured workflow with bid seasons, selection windows, seniority or lottery rules, policy limits, officer choices, and approved calendar placement.

Common problem
  • Bidding cycles can be hard to manage fairly when they depend on spreadsheets or manual lists.
  • Policy caps and bid order rules are easy to misread or apply inconsistently.
  • Bidding can take a long time when it depends on spreadsheets or manual lists.
  • Approved bid outcomes must often be re-entered somewhere else.
Officer Understands bid windows, order, choices, and results more clearly.
Supervisor Runs the process with fewer manual checks and fewer disputes.
Command Sees the final vacation picture earlier and can plan staffing around it.
Training and availability

Training Tracker

Training Tracker helps agencies plan training days, see roster impact, assign personnel, and keep training-related status visible to supervisors and command staff.

Common problem
  • Training assignments affect daily coverage but may be tracked separately.
  • Supervisors can lose time tracking individual attendance to training.
  • An officer may be on the street without their required annual training.
Officer Sees assigned training and status more clearly.
Supervisor Gives supervisors a simpler way to assign training and track attendance.
Command Reduces liability by helping confirm officers have required annual training.
Assignments and paid details

Extra Duty / Pay Jobs

Extra Duty organizes outside requests, supervisor review, conflict checks, officer assignment, notifications, audit history, and billing exports.

Common problem
  • Requests can arrive through phone calls, emails, forms, or informal contacts.
  • Assignments may be questioned if the process is not visible or documented.
  • Billing and audit support can require manual reconstruction.
Officer Gets a clearer path to available work and assignment notifications.
Supervisor Vets requests, checks conflicts, assigns fairly, and keeps a record.
Command Reviews detail activity, fairness, accountability, and billing support.
Records and custody history

Tow Logs

Tow companies are assigned unit designators and can submit tow sheets with the required information for private tows and repossessions. The officer's role is simplified to running the vehicle through NCIC and verifying the entry.

Common problem
  • Tow documentation can be scattered across paper, forms, email, or records systems.
  • Searching for a vehicle, tow company, officer, or custody event can take time.
  • Records staff and command need cleaner documentation for review.
Officer Completes tow entries with clearer required fields.
Supervisor Reviews tow activity and verifies required documentation.
Command Gets searchable history and exports for records, audits, and accountability.
Public input workflow

Anonymous Tips

Anonymous Tips gives agencies a controlled intake workflow for public information, with triage, tasking, status tracking, and audit-ready reporting.

Common problem
  • Tips can arrive through disconnected channels and lose context.
  • Follow-up tasks may be hard to track across shifts or units.
  • Leadership may not have a clear picture of intake, action, and closure.
Officer Receives clearer assignments when a tip requires follow-up.
Supervisor Triages tips, assigns action, and tracks progress.
Command Reviews tip volume, status, outcomes, and accountability reports.
05 / People

Role perspectives

G3 is designed so each rank gets value in the way they actually work: easy-to-use tools for officers, safer and faster workflows for supervisors, and clearer oversight for command staff.

Officers

Officers need tools that are easy to use during real daily work.

  • Check schedules, assignments, and vacation availability without chasing information.
  • Submit requests and see status from wherever they are working.
  • Get clear training instructions, reporting locations, and fair access to opportunities.

Supervisors

Supervisors need control of staffing, schedules, assets, and assignments in order to increase officer safety.

  • Approve requests with a full view of coverage, turnover, rules, and available assets.
  • Assign training, details, and follow-up tasks with less back-and-forth.
  • See coverage gaps earlier so staffing decisions are made before safety is affected.

Command staff

Command staff need operational visibility that supports reducing liability.

  • See staffing levels, coverage gaps, leave patterns, and turnover risk before they become incidents.
  • Confirm that required training, assignments, and operational records are documented.
  • Use audit history to explain decisions and reduce liability after questions arise.

Administrative stakeholders

Administrative stakeholders need safer shifts, stronger documentation, and more officer time focused on public safety.

  • Reduce administrative work so officers spend less time managing paperwork.
  • Support safer shifts through better staffing visibility.
  • Keep documentation cleaner for records, review, and continuity.
